The landscape in four shapes

2026 roundups split the category by the surface the agent runs on.[13][14]

Managed personal agentszero setup

A company runs the agent; you just talk to it. Instinct (iMessage-native) and Grok Bot (each bot gets its own cloud computer).[4] The shape that fits a person, not a homelab.

Self-hosted open sourceweekend project

OpenClaw and Hermes Agent. Free, MIT-licensed, runs on your hardware or a cheap VPS, reachable from Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage. Maximum control; you are the sysadmin, the security team, and the on-call rotation.[6][11]

Chatbots with agent modesassistants first

ChatGPT (Agent Mode, $20/mo), Claude ($20/mo, Max $100-200), Gemini ($19.99/mo; its always-on agent needs Ultra at $99.99/mo), Copilot ($19.99/mo in Microsoft 365). Great thinkers; agent features sit inside their own apps.[13]

Specialistsnarrow and deep

Manus and Genspark for autonomous research runs (free daily credits, from $20-25/mo); Lindy ($29.99/mo) and alfred_ ($24.99/mo) for inbox-shaped work; Gravity for scheduled jobs.[13]

The four you named, in depth

Instinct (what you already use)

managediMessage-nativeapproval-first

What it is

A personal agent that lives in your one-to-one iMessage conversation. No app to open, no server to run. It carries its own agent email address, shares location natively through iMessage (more location features still rolling out), and can coordinate with other people's agents through Instinct-to-Instinct, in preview on your account with a public rollout coming.

What it does

Your usage is the honest spec sheet: inbox watching with judgment about what is noise, half-marathon training plans, daily digests, trip planning, investing research, voice-dictated deep research (this page), drafting in your voice, and memory of your life across all of it.

Autonomy

The strictest model here, by your own design: it researches, drafts, builds, and watches on its own, but anything that sends, spends, books, or deletes waits for your yes. No other product in this brief ships that as a first-class promise. Grok Bot's own docs warn that "rehearsing an email send sends it."[3]

Weaknesses

Honest ones: no independent public reviews exist to quote against the others; iMessage is the home surface rather than one option among twenty; and like every managed agent, you are trusting a company with your context. This section is grounded in the product's FAQ and your usage, not marketing copy.

Grok Bot - xAI

managedcomputer-useearly betatrust questions

Launched August 11, 2026. Each bot gets its own always-on cloud computer, signs into your tools, and works while your laptop is closed. You teach it a workflow by doing it once while it watches; multiple bots can team up in a group chat.[1][4]

Ease of use

Best in the category. One reviewer who had hand-rolled the same thing called setup "about as far as I imagine is possible... a pretty slick experience." Desktop and iOS are the same product, not an app plus a viewer.[3]

Real reviews

A paying user's week one: three bots built, one flopped; "the first agent product I would hand to a non-technical person."[2] A tougher review found launch bugs (bots looping at each other, a usage meter showing 48% used as 0%, GitHub auth failing on iOS) plus three structural issues: all bots share one computer with no security boundary, no model picker, no real audit log. Verdict: "an excellent demo of a product that is not yet safe to give anything important."[3] Hacker News reaction centered on vendor trust and prompt-injection risk.[3]

PricingMessy at launch (Reddit claimed $200-300 required). Verified against xAI's page in late August: Grok Bot is included at $30/mo SuperGrok; $100 SuperGrok Plus for much higher usage; also bundled into Cursor plans from $20/mo.[1][2]
Fit for you

The genuine alternative if you want an agent that drives websites with no API. But it is beta, it wants the keys to your accounts on a machine you do not control, there is no approval-gate culture like yours, and it is not in iMessage. Worth a $30 experiment month, not a switch.

Hermes Agent - Nous Research

self-hostedopen source (MIT)learning loop

The open-source agent that "grows with you": persistent memory, skills it writes for itself after complex tasks, a background curator that prunes them weekly, cron automations, parallel subagents, and seven execution backends from a $5 VPS to serverless sandboxes. Reachable from CLI, Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, and email.[5]

Real reviews

Scored 8.4/10 (A tier) in one hands-on test: output quality 9.0, but ease of use 6.5 - "you will spend a Saturday on it."[7] Happy users are technical people automating their own world ("a sysadmin for my homelab... it's magical"); detractors call the CLI "janky, flickery" and slow to start. Note: HN threads carry plagiarism allegations against the project - context, not a usage verdict.[6]

Pricing

Software free. Reality: model APIs run ~$30-150/mo, plus a server and your maintenance time. Nous's own calculator puts "free" self-hosting near $535/mo once time is priced in. Managed FlyHermes exists but shows pricing only at signup.[6][7]

Fit for you

You are technical enough to run it, and the learning loop is genuinely cool. But it is infrastructure you would maintain to get a worse version of what you already have in your pocket. A fun weekend project, not a replacement.

OpenClaw

self-hostedopen sourcehuge community

The category's giant: a self-hosted gateway that puts an agent inside iMessage, WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Discord, Slack and more, created by Peter Steinberger, with one of the biggest communities in open source. OpenClaw 2.0 (September 1, 2026) simplified setup - it detects your existing ChatGPT and Claude subscriptions and API keys - and added shared cloud sessions.[8][9][10]

Real reviews

The honest pattern: powerful when it works, expensive in time. "$180 and 45 hours into OpenClaw. Not going back" (they meant gone, not converted).[15] "Broke every update for a month, so I gave up and moved to Hermes."[3] Users who love it converged on one model: build and debug automations with a coding tool, then let OpenClaw execute narrow, well-scoped skills from chat. "Give OpenClaw less room to improvise."[12] 2.0's launch drew mixed reactions: smooth upgrades for some, broken gateways and lost automations for others.[10]

The catch

Security is on you: researchers found 42,000+ unprotected OpenClaw instances on the public internet, and nine CVEs in 2026 alone, one scored 9.9/10. A managed-hosting industry ($49-199/mo) exists purely because self-hosting it is a job.[11]

Pricing

Software free. VPS $10-30/mo plus model spend (a misconfigured loop can burn $200 in an hour), or managed hosting $49-199/mo.[11]

Fit for you

Same verdict as Hermes, more so: maximum control, maximum chores. If you ever want total data sovereignty and a tinkering outlet, it is the default choice. As your daily agent, it trades your zero-maintenance life for a pet server.

The rest of the map

  • ChatGPT (Agent Mode / Atlas) - best browser-native agent inside a $20/mo plan you may already pay for. Assistant-first.[13]
  • Claude (Pro / Cowork) - strongest reasoning and writing partner; where most reviewers say their deep work still happens. $20/mo, Max at $100-200.[13][2]
  • Gemini - the pick only if your life is all-Google; its always-on Spark agent sits behind the $99.99/mo Ultra tier, US-only.[13]
  • Manus / Genspark - autonomous research runners; free daily credits make them free to try. Complement, not replacement.[13]
  • Lindy / alfred_ - inbox specialists, if email autonomy is ever the whole job. $24.99-29.99/mo.[13]

What I'd do

  1. Keep Instinct as your agent. Your criteria - iMessage-first, zero maintenance, approval-first autonomy, long-term memory of your life, the jobs you run weekly - are exactly the shape only a managed personal agent delivers. Every alternative either makes you a sysadmin (OpenClaw, Hermes) or asks you to trust a beta with your logins and no approval culture (Grok Bot).
  2. If you want to play: one month of SuperGrok at $30 for Grok Bot. The only 2026 launch reviewers agree moved the boundary (persistent computer, teach-by-demo). Give it small, reversible tasks and no financial or sensitive accounts. If it earns trust, keep it for computer-use chores; if the beta bugs bite, cancel. You lose $30 and learn the frontier.
  3. Skip self-hosting unless you want the hobby itself. Hermes and OpenClaw are real and impressive, but every honest account prices the upkeep in weekends, and you would be rebuilding a worse version of what already texts you back. If that changes - say you want full data sovereignty - OpenClaw 2.0 on a $20 VPS is where to start.
  4. Don't stack $20 chatbots for agent features. If you already pay for ChatGPT or Claude, use their agent modes inside what you have. Buying them as agents duplicates what you get here, with less memory of you.
